## Claim strength

- **Direct quantitative evidence:** state the value, direction, units, conditions, and source.
- **Complete visual sequence:** state the visible trend and affected response; keep numbers in the original axes, legends, tables, or data band.
- **Mechanistic consistency:** use wording such as “表明”, “与……一致”, or “可能与……有关”; identify the missing stress, flux, rate, or control evidence.
- **Hypothesis or next step:** label it as a proposal. Do not place it in the same conclusion band as an observed result.

The conclusion band should answer “what changes and under which condition?” It should not merely repeat the slide title or list parameters. Detailed conclusions belong in arrow bullets and speaker notes.

## Result and discussion page contract

Every result or discussion slide contains three linked layers:

1. **Page-level claim:** the most important finding or mechanism statement beneath the title. Use one sentence by default, two compact bullets for two parallel findings, or one longer statement when the causal or conditional relationship would otherwise be lost.
2. **Evidence area:** original figures, tables, images, or source-grounded native diagrams at a readable size. The evidence arrangement follows its comparison structure rather than a fixed slide grid.
3. **Evidence-group explanation:** a short explanation adjacent to every distinct figure group. State what is compared, the changed parameter, the visual cue to inspect, the response direction, and the strongest interpretation supported by the source.

Do not make the local explanation duplicate the page-level claim. The page-level claim synthesizes the slide; the local explanation teaches the audience how the displayed evidence supports that synthesis.

## Reading a result slide

Make the slide self-explanatory:

1. identify fixed conditions;
2. identify the changed parameter;
3. map every row, column, color, line, panel letter, and time point;
4. state what the audience should compare;
5. state what visual cue to inspect: slope, peak, contour width, interface shape, arrival time, oscillation amplitude, or another source-supported feature;
6. separate morphology, concentration/field distribution, and overall response when those are the relevant dimensions;
7. state the mechanism only to the strength supported by the source.

Do not create fake curves, extrapolate missing cases, digitize approximate values without approval, or convert correlation into certain causation.
